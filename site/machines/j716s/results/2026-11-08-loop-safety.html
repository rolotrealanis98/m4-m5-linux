Why: the Thunderbolt T0 kernel hung in init with the watchdog already handed to Linux, so the machine never reset (see the T0 result). Test payloads now carry watchdog.open_timeout=300 and an initramfs opener that pets /dev/watchdog0 until userspace takes over; a kernel that reaches userspace but never opens the device is reset by the core and stage 1 falls back. Error stage cleared before each boot, ESP_SETTLE=120.

v1 (03:22 UTC), for the record #

Positive wdt-opener (2daadc5b, watchdog.open_timeout=300, initramfs opener): booted, m5-selftest "network OK", up past 400 s with no reset, clean systemctl reboot back on the primary slot. The hand-over to systemd did not happen: no m5pro-watchdog.conf drop-in, RuntimeWatchdogUSec=0, "Failed to open watchdog device /dev/watchdog0: Device or resource busy" at shutdown and the kernel "watchdog did not stop!". v2 fixed this (the pre-switch_root block, kmsg logging, the 0x31 stage mark). The v1 negative twin was inconclusive (the fallback came in the 75-169 s band, with no journal of the twin) and was rerun in v2 with the stage marks.

Side effect (intended) #

The drop-in now lives on the shared root, so the daily runs with RuntimeWatchdogSec=60: a PID 1 stall of over 60 s resets the machine. s2idle is handled by the watchdog core's PM notifier and apple_wdt suspend/resume.

Not tested #

A lid-close sleep with systemd owning the watchdog (the case that matters on the daily; attended). The positive twin's own self-test failed once (ping of the fixed check address failed although wlan0 had associated at 6.5 s); one occurrence, not understood, unrelated to the watchdog.
