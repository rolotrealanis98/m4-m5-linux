What makes unattended testing safe on the J716s, and the one rule it needs.

The safety net #

Two-stage m1n1 with an A/B stage 1: after a failed boot (counted by iBoot's boot-failure counter) stage 1 boots the previous payload; a 150 s watchdog covers a hang before Linux takes it over; test payloads revert themselves when the network does not come up within 90 s. Over 7 November it brought the machine back from every failed test payload without a person, except the Thunderbolt hang (see that result) and a loader that disables the watchdog (never put on the boot slot unattended).

RESTART-EXP4: the cause of "the first boot of a fresh payload falls back" #

Three times: dd if=/dev/zero of=/root/burst bs=1M count=70 conv=fsync on the ext4 root, then systemctl reboot within 1 s, no ESP write at all. Arm 1: fallback-after-failure, error stage 0x30 (the next boot of the unchanged payload died in early Linux and the twin booted). Arms 2 and 3: clean. Then three times: rewrite the same payload to the ESP, wait 60 s, reboot: all clean.

Conclusion: rebooting while the SSD is still busy after a write burst makes the next boot fail early (0x30) or counts the restart as a failure (0x00), independent of the FAT ESP. Rule: 60 s of idle after any large write before a reboot. With the settle: 9 of 9 clean that day; with a ~1 s gap, 5 of 14 failed. The ESP write helper defaults to the 60 s settle.

RESTART-EXP2 and EXP3 #

Plain systemctl reboot cycles without a write, from the power-kernel and the omacom-loader sessions: 3 of 3 and 3 of 3 clean (primary slot).

CRASHLOG-PRAM: no RAM-backed crash log #

A ramoops region carved from iBoot's panic-log region registered, but both a warm reboot and a deliberate panic left the store empty on the next boot: iBoot consumes and clears that region and the DRAM is zeroed. Evidence from a frozen boot has to come from outside the machine (netconsole).

Not tested #

The stage-1 change that marks boot stages so a counted restart boots the primary (built, waiting for an attended sitting).
